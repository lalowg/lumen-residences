      04

      Six materials, chosen for how they take the light

      The palette is short on purpose. Each surface was tested at the window, at seven in the morning and again at six in the evening, before it was allowed into a room.

      Board-marked concrete

      Used in Lobby walls, stair core, loggia soffits

      Poured against rough-sawn larch boards, so every wall carries the grain of the timber that shaped it. Low sun picks out each board line.

      Section, not to scale. Sun angles are illustrative.

      05

      The building, in section

      Ground and level 1

      A lobby that opens to the courtyard

      A five-metre ground floor runs straight through from the quay to a planted courtyard, so you can see daylight at the far end the moment you step inside. Post, bicycles and a residents' library sit along the north wall.

      Levels 2–5

      The Atelier and the first Courtyards

      Compact homes with three-metre ceilings and full-height glazing. In winter the low sun reaches the back wall of the living room; deep south balconies keep the high summer sun on the terrace instead of the floor.

      Levels 6–9

      Courtyards above the treeline

      Above the plane trees on the quay the views open to the water. Each Courtyard residence is dual-aspect, with a study that catches the last of the afternoon.

      Levels 10–12

      The Meridian, set back

      The top three levels step back from both facades. The setback becomes a terrace on the south side and lets the floors below keep their sky. Ceilings rise to 3.2 metres.

      Roof

      A garden for every resident

      Multi-stem birch, a long table and a glasshouse for winter evenings. It is the one place in the building where the sun is never in someone else's room.
